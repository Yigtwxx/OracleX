Election and appointment markets resolve on a procedure, not on a mood. Weigh
polling against its own track record in that jurisdiction, and treat the
mechanics — filing deadlines, ballot access, delegate rules, who certifies the
result — as the thing most likely to decide a close question. A candidate ahead
in the polls who has missed a filing deadline is not ahead.

Poll numbers are evidence about a sample on a date, not about the outcome. Cite
the date and the pollster. A single survey moving the average is usually noise.

Incumbency, turnout patterns and the resolution date's distance from the vote
matter more than the current sentiment of coverage.
