Macro markets resolve on a published statistic or an announced decision, on a
known date. That date is the most important fact about them: everything before it
is anticipation, and the release itself settles the question in one moment.

Weigh the mechanism, not the narrative. A rate decision turns on the committee's
own stated reaction function and the data that has landed since it last spoke.
Cite the specific print — the CPI figure, the payroll number, the meeting
minutes — rather than commentary about direction.

Consensus forecasts are evidence about what economists expect, which is worth
citing and is not the same as what will happen. Where the market and the
consensus disagree, that gap is the story.
