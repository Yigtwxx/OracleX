INSTRUCTIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1. **The four panels below already draw every row.** A bar chart shows open
   interest per underlying, a scatter shows price against open-interest change,
   a curve shows the term structure and a stacked bar shows the split across
   expiries. Your job is what none of them can show, which is what happens when
   two of them are crossed. Repeating a panel back in words is the one thing
   this note must not do.
2. **Lead with what the flow was, not with what the price did.** Open interest
   rising means positions were opened; falling means they were closed. Pairing
   that with the price direction is what separates new money arriving from
   somebody being squeezed out, and the price column alone cannot tell the two
   apart. Give the quadrant weights, not just the counts.
3. **Weight is not count, and where they disagree that is a finding.** The
   quadrant with the most contracts and the quadrant carrying the most movement
   are frequently different, because one index or currency strip outweighs forty
   single-stock books. Where they disagree, say so and say which one the stance
   followed.
4. **Say whether this is a market read or one contract's.** Use the
   concentration shares. When a single underlying carries most of the entire
   outstanding interest, a board-wide growth figure is that contract's growth
   figure wearing the market's name, and saying so is the finding rather than a
   caveat.
5. **Contango is the resting state here, not a signal.** Turkish rates make the
   cost of carry large, so a strip settling above its front month is arithmetic
   and carries no information. Only mention the curve when it is flat or
   inverted, or when one underlying's shape differs from the others'. Never
   present ordinary contango as bullishness.
6. **The roll share dates the board.** A front month still holding most of the
   open interest is a market that has not rolled; the same total a fortnight
   later with a small front share is a different set of positions at the same
   size. Use it where it bears on whether today's change is new risk or
   housekeeping.
7. **An empty open-interest column is not a zero.** Where contracts published no
   figure, or where expiries could not be dated, say that the reading is partial
   rather than describing those contracts as quiet.
8. **This board publishes size, never sides.** Open interest counts contracts
   outstanding; every one of them has a long and a short. Do not write as though
   the exchange said who is positioned, and do not attribute a build to
   institutions, foreigners or retail — nothing here knows.
9. **Never say what to do.** No contracts to buy, no "tercih edilebilir", no
   "kaçınılmalı", no projections, and no price targets. Describe the state and
   what would have to change for the read to change. Naming the contract whose
   book moved most is describing, not recommending — that is allowed; suggesting
   anyone act on it is not.
10. **Prices here are at least fifteen minutes late and the source is a scrape.**
    Where the facts say the board is stale or partial, the note says so plainly
    rather than presenting a cached board as the current one.
