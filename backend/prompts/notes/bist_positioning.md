INSTRUCTIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1. **The four panels below already draw every row.** The scatter shows float
   against volume, the histogram shows the 52-week range, the treemap shows
   sector heat and the quadrant chart shows futures. Your job is what none of
   them can show, which is what happens when two of them are crossed. Repeating
   a panel back in words is the one thing this note must not do.
2. **Lead with the crowd against the board.** Whether the busiest names sit
   higher or lower in their own year than the median listing is the read, and it
   is the difference between a market buying what is already working and one
   buying what has already fallen. Give the two range figures and the gap
   between them.
3. **Say what kind of company the crowding is happening in.** Compare the
   cohort's median free float against the board's. A crowd concentrated in
   narrow-float names is a different condition from the same volume spread
   across liquid ones, because the same flow moves a tight float much further —
   one clause on that mechanism, where the figures raise it.
4. **Say whether the crowding is a market condition or one sector's.** Use the
   sector shares. When a single sector carries most of the board's whole
   crowding score, the ranking is that sector's story and saying so is the
   finding.
5. **Read the range against RSI, not on its own.** A cohort sitting at its highs
   whose median RSI has fallen back has stopped being bought without having been
   sold. Where the two disagree, say which and in which direction.
6. **Treat the futures block as a sample, not as the market.** Roughly forty
   underlyings have contracts against several hundred listings, so what the
   quadrants say is a statement about those names. Say that when you use it, and
   name the quadrant the count actually favours rather than the one the price
   direction suggests.
7. **A reading that is "not available" is stated, not skipped.** Where VİOP could
   not be read, say that published positioning is missing from this read rather
   than describing the board as though futures agreed with it.
8. **The board cannot see which funds hold which share.** That was what this page
   was specified as; no public source carries it, so nothing here says who is
   positioned — only how much of a company can trade, how unusual its volume is,
   where it sits in its year, and what its futures did. Say this once, plainly,
   where it bears on a claim you are making. Do not present the board as a view
   of institutional flow.
9. **Never say what to do.** No names to buy, no "tercih edilebilir", no
   "kaçınılmalı", no projections, and no price targets. Describe the state and
   what would have to change for the read to change. Naming a share the facts
   gave you as the clearest example of a crowded, narrow-float move is
   describing, not recommending — that is allowed; suggesting anyone act on it is
   not.
10. **Crowding is a sorting aid, not a verdict.** It is relative volume divided
    by free float and nothing more. Do not write it up as a proprietary signal,
    and do not treat a high score as evidence of anyone's intent.
